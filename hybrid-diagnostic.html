Healthcare · Live

One booking for a complete full-body checkup

In most health apps, a "full body checkup" means blood tests with home sample collection, and nothing else. I redesigned the diagnostic booking flow so a full-body checkup includes radiology too. Users can book pathology and radiology in one booking, with pathology at home or at a centre.

01The problem

Most "full body checkups" left out radiology

Most health apps sell "full body checkup" packages that only include pathology tests with home sample collection. A complete checkup also needs radiology, and radiology can't be done at home.

This left users with two options, and neither was good:

  1. Two separate bookings: pathology at home, radiology at a centre. Far more users completed pathology than radiology.
  2. One combined package that needed a centre visit for everything, so users lost home collection.

Users who wanted home sample collection had to go to a centre and wait. That took time out of their day for something that should have been quick.

What I wanted to solve

How might we let users book a complete checkup, pathology and radiology, in a single booking, while still letting them choose home or centre for pathology?

02Constraints

What made this hard

PartnersNo single lab does both

No lab partner offers both home pathology collection and centre-based radiology together.

PhysicalRadiology needs a centre

Radiology tests can't be performed at home. They need special machines.

SchedulingNo single time slot

A combined package can't have one time slot for both pathology and radiology.

BackendTwo orders underneath

Pathology and radiology come from different labs, so each runs as its own order with its own status.

03Root cause

The barrier was the forced centre visit

It started with funnel data. My PM noticed that packages with radiology converted noticeably worse than pathology-only packages. I went through the data with my PM to confirm the pattern, and it held.

These checkups are employer-covered benefits, so users don't pay for them. Cost wasn't the reason. Something else was stopping users from booking radiology.

The system assumed every booking was either fully at home or fully at a centre. When a package had both pathology and radiology, it couldn't split them. So it picked the stricter option and sent users to a centre for everything.

Hypothesis

If we keep pathology at home, send users to a centre only for radiology, and put both in one booking, hybrid packages should complete at a rate closer to pathology-only packages.

04Borrowing a pattern

Booking two labs like a round-trip flight

The biggest challenge was on the partner side. No single lab partner offered both home pathology and centre radiology. So the fix had to combine two partner labs into one booking.

There was no existing pattern in diagnostics to borrow. A stakeholder suggested looking at how travel apps like MakeMyTrip handle round trips, where two separate flights are booked in one checkout.

We used the same model: two lab partners instead of two flights, combined into one order and priced as a single combo.

05The solution

The redesigned booking flow

The combo card

The Lab Listing screen is the core of the redesign. No single lab could do everything, so the screen shows around fifteen partner combinations instead. Each one pairs a pathology lab with a radiology lab, sorted by distance and shown as a single card.

Users were being asked to trust two lab brands at once, and some of them were unfamiliar. So I added trust signals to the card itself: NABL and ISO accreditation badges on each partner logo, and instant-confirmation tags.

In the backend, each combo was two separate sub-orders under one parent order. If one partner needed to reschedule, the other half of the booking was not affected.

06Slots, review and status

Two sets of steps in one booking

Slot selection, order review and order status follow the same idea. It is one order, with separate steps for pathology and radiology.

For slot selection, I reused the bottom sheet users already knew from single-package bookings. It has two tabs in order, pathology first, radiology second, and each one gets a tick when confirmed before the user moves on.

Order review and order status show both parts clearly, but always present them as a single booking.

07Where it stands

Live, with results still to come

This flow is live, but it is not fully validated yet. I expect that one order with two guided steps, instead of two separate checkouts, will reduce drop-off.

Once it has been live for a while, these three signals will show whether it worked:

Signal 01Hybrid package adoption

Are more users choosing packages that include radiology?

Signal 02Fulfilment completion

Do both halves of the booking actually get completed?

Signal 03Less split-order drop-off

Do fewer users stop after pathology and skip radiology?
