Family health checkups · 2026

Letting employees add their family to the checkup they've already booked

An offer to add family members to an employer-sponsored annual health checkup, placed after the booking so it doesn't get in the way.

The company gives employees a sponsored annual health checkup (AHC). Many of them also want checkups for their parents or partner. I designed a way to add family members to the same home visit, at a family price, without slowing down the employee's own booking.

My role

End-to-end Product Designer
From the first booking concept to the post-booking flow, patient selection and tiered pricing.

Key decisions

  1. Offer family after booking, not during it
  2. Add them to the same visit, not a new order
  3. Show family prices as clear tiers

Early results

32.6% adoption
18.9% conversion rate

Product analytics, Sep 19 – Oct 18, 2026.

01 The brief

A health benefit that stops at the employee

An employer-sponsored AHC covers the employee. But people don't only care about their own health. Business data showed that some users also wanted checkups for their family.

The company wanted to grow lab revenue by selling paid AHC packages to those family members.

The hard part was the timing: introducing the offer without getting in the way of the employee's own booking.

For users

  • Book checkups for family with less back-and-forth
  • Use the same home visit they already booked
  • Pay a family price, and only for the extra members

For the business

  • More paid AHC bookings
  • More lab revenue from each sponsored booking
  • A higher average order value

The problem we set out to solve

How might we help employees extend their sponsored checkup to their family, and open a new revenue stream, without making the original booking harder?

The goalKeep the sponsored booking simple, then make adding family feel like a natural next step.

Constraints

  1. The sponsored booking comes firstEmployees should finish their own booking without making pricing decisions they don't need.
  2. Every lab prices differentlyLab partners have their own prices and their own family discounts.
  3. One visit, not a new orderFamily members had to join the lab and home visit the employee already chose.
  4. Real patient profilesThe flow had to support picking saved family profiles, adding a new patient, and paying for extra members.

02 Research

Competitors sell family discounts before booking

I looked at how PharmEasy, Healthians and Tata 1mg offer family discounts. All three show them before the booking, while people are still shopping for a checkup.

Our users arrive differently. They come to use a benefit their employer already paid for, not to shop. Copying the same placement would add a decision before their main task was done.

Based on business data and competitor analysis. We didn't run user interviews or usability tests for this project.

Competitor apps

  1. Browse packages
  2. Family discount
  3. Pick a lab
  4. Book

Shopping for a checkup. A discount helps them decide.

Our users

  1. Use my sponsored AHC
  2. Pick a lab
  3. Book
  4. Add family?

Using a benefit. The offer works better once the booking is done.

What competitors do

All four ask about family before the first booking is done. For someone using a sponsored benefit, that's one more decision in the way.

The hypothesis

Let the employee finish the sponsored booking first. Then offer a simple way to add family to that same visit.

  1. Book my sponsored AHC
  2. See the offer on my order
  3. Add family to the same visit

03 Key decision

Decision 01

Offer family after the booking, not during it

  1. First idea

    Add family while booking

    A "Booking for family?" block on the package page with 1, 2 and 3+ member prices, then lab cards showing what you'd pay for everyone.

  2. Problem

    Too many decisions at once

    Users would have to compare labs, different prices and discounts, and covered versus paid members, just to book their own checkup.

  3. Decision

    Split the two tasks

    The employee books their sponsored AHC first. The family offer appears afterwards, on the order timeline.

  4. Shipped

    A post-booking add-on

    The lab is already chosen, so family pricing is a separate, smaller decision.

Rejected concept

Family pricing during booking

  • Decide on family before booking your own checkup
  • Compare labs on family prices, not just on convenience
  • 3 choices before your own booking is done

Shipped concept

Family offer after booking

  • Book your own checkup with one choice: the lab
  • See the family offer on the order, with the time left before pickup
  • 1 choice before your own booking is done

Why I rejected the first concept

  1. The user's intent is their own checkupThe AHC is sponsored, so people come to book their free package first. That's the goal they arrive with.
  2. More steps before that goalAsking about family in the middle of booking adds steps between the user and the checkup they came for.
  3. Pricing gets complicated fastEvery lab has its own family price and offer. Showing each one for every member count on a single lab listing page makes it hard to read, and hard to manage.
Options
  • Add family during package and lab selection
  • Offer family after the sponsored booking
Chose
Offer family after booking, on the order timeline, reusing the chosen lab and visit
Trade-off
Users see family pricing later in the journey. In return, the sponsored booking stays simple.

I didn't add more information to help with a complex decision. I moved the decision to a better moment.

01 · Offer

User needs
To know the option exists, while there's still time.
Product does
"Get family tested in this visit" sits on the order status, under the booking. It says what stays the same (slot, address, no extra trip) and how long is left before the pickup.

Shows: the offer appears where the booking already lives.

02 · Benefits

User needs
To understand what extending means.
Product does
Three short points: one trip for the whole family, a family price, and no double charge for the booking that's already covered. The lab and slot are shown, so nothing has to be picked again.

03 · Select

User needs
To see the cost before choosing anyone.
Product does
Price tiers for 1, 2 and 3 extra members, each with the price per person. The employee is already in the list and can't be removed.

04 · Add

User needs
To add someone who isn't saved yet.
Product does
A short form that starts with the relationship, so adding a parent or child is a couple of taps.

05 · Selected

User needs
To know the saving is real.
Product does
"2 members selected" and "You are saving ₹1200" update as people are picked.

Shows: the price is never a surprise.

06 · Review

User needs
To see exactly what they're paying for.
Product does
Same lab, address and slot. The employee is marked "Paid", the family are "Newly Added", and the bill only covers the add-ons.

Shows: one visit for the whole family.

Family pricing, in tiers

The more family members join the visit, the lower the price per person. The tiers sit at the top of the patient list, so the cost is clear before anyone is picked.

  • +1 member: ₹4,000
  • +2 members: ₹6,800, or ₹3,400 each
  • +3 members: ₹9,600, or ₹3,200 each

Prices from the shipped screens, for the Full Body Premium Plus package.

06 Learnings

What I took from this project

  1. Design for the way people arrive. A pattern that works for shoppers may not work for someone using an employer benefit.
  2. Sometimes the fix isn't more information. It's moving the decision to a better moment.
  3. Reusing what the user already chose, here the lab and the visit, makes an extra purchase feel small.
  4. Shipping is the start. The funnel showed exactly where to look next.

Book your own checkup first.
Then bring your family along.

I split one crowded decision into two simple ones: the employee books their sponsored checkup, then adds family to the same visit at a family price.
